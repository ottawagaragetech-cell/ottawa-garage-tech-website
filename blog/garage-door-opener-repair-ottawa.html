Quick checks before you assume the opener is dead

  • Power — confirm the opener is plugged in and the ceiling outlet has power. After storms, GFCI outlets in the garage often trip
  • Lock button — many wall controls have a lock that ignores remotes and keypads; the wall button still works
  • Remote batteries — try the wall button. If the wall works and remotes do not, it is usually batteries, programming, or a failed remote — not the motor
  • Photo-eye sensors — look for a solid vs blinking LED on each sensor. Bins, bikes, and autumn leaves often break the beam (see our won't-close guide)
  • Door balance — if the door suddenly feels heavy, the opener may be fine and a spring may have failed. Stop using the opener

Do not crank opener force settings to “make it close.” That hides a real problem and can crush something in the path.

What common opener symptoms mean

Opener lights flash, door will not close

Usually safety sensors out of alignment, a dirty lens, or a blocked beam. Clean both sensors, make sure they face each other, and keep the wires from getting snagged on storage.

Motor hums or clicks, door barely moves

Worn gears (common on older chain-drive units), a slipped carriage, or a door that is out of balance. Continuing to hit the remote can strip the remaining gear teeth.

Remote works only close to the door

Weak remote battery, antenna issue, or interference. Less often, a failing logic board. Try a fresh battery and the wall control first.

Door opens, then slams or will not stay up

That is almost never “just the opener.” Springs and cables carry the weight — the opener is meant to lift a balanced door. Book an inspection before the next cycle.

Grinding from the opener rail

Dry chain, worn sprocket, or a failing gear set. Belt-drive units are quieter but still need sensor and limit checks — especially in townhomes where bedrooms sit above the garage. See noisy garage door fixes.
